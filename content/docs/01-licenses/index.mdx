## The importance of Licenses [step]

Licenses are important because they define the terms under which others can use, modify, and distribute your work. They protect both the creator and the users by clearly outlining rights and responsibilities on the same they provide clarity and legal protection about:

- Usage rights
- Modification rights
- Distribution rights
- Attribution requirements
- Liability limitations
- Monetization permissions and Revenue sharing
- Transferability of ownership and sublicense rights

So, it helps to ensure that **your work** is used in a way that aligns with **your intentions and values**.

## Where to find Licenses [step]

Licenses can be found and automatically added to projects when using tools like **GitHub**, **GitLab**, **Bitbucket** or other version control systems.

They have a huge list of licenses available, and you can choose the one that best fits your needs, which are accessible when you are creating a new repository.

Other resources for licenses include:

- [x] Websites with license generators and templates, being the most common [Choose a License](https://choosealicense.com/)
- [x] [My public repository](https://github.com/code-with-von/license-templates), where I share license templates I use in my projects
- [x] Custom license agreements created with the help of a professional lawyer

## About Licenses of Use [step]

Usually called "End User License Agreements" (EULAs), these are agreements that define the terms of use for software, services, or other digital products. They are typically used to protect the intellectual property of the creator and to ensure that the user understands the terms of use.

Even though they are not as common as other types of licenses, they are still important to have in place to protect the intellectual property of the creator and to ensure that the user understands the terms of use, and preferentially connected to other types of licenses.

In this project, for example, our **EULA** is connected to **GNU General Public License v3.0**.

- [x] The **EULA** sets my **ideals**, **rights** and **responsibilities** as the creator of this template that should be followed by the parties (the users and I) should follow and understand
- [x] The **GNU** specifies the terms of use for this template with more details
